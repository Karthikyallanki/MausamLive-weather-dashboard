# Air Quality Index (AQI) Classification & Health Guidance

## Understanding the Air Quality Index (AQI)
The Air Quality Index (AQI) reports daily air quality levels and communicates how clean or polluted the surrounding air is, along with associated health effects.

## AQI Categories & Health Implications

### Good (AQI 0 - 50)
- **Air Quality:** Satisfactory; little to no health risk.
- **Guidance:** Ideal for all outdoor activities and physical exercise.

### Moderate (AQI 51 - 100)
- **Air Quality:** Acceptable. However, unusually sensitive individuals (e.g., severe asthmatics) may experience mild respiratory symptoms.
- **Guidance:** Sensitive groups should monitor for symptoms during heavy outdoor exertion.

### Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (AQI 101 - 150)
- **Air Quality:** Members of sensitive groups (children, elderly, people with lung or heart disease) may experience health effects.
- **Guidance:** Sensitive individuals should reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. General public is unlikely to be affected.

### Unhealthy (AQI 151 - 200)
- **Air Quality:** Everyone may begin to experience adverse health effects; sensitive groups may experience more serious symptoms.
- **Guidance:** Active adults, children, and people with respiratory disease should avoid prolonged outdoor exertion. Wearing an N95 mask outdoors is recommended.

### Very Unhealthy (AQI 201 - 300)
- **Air Quality:** Health alert: risk of health effects for the entire population.
- **Guidance:** Everyone should limit outdoor activities. Keep windows closed and operate indoor air purifiers.

### Hazardous (AQI 301+)
- **Air Quality:** Health warnings of emergency conditions.
- **Guidance:** Remain indoors, keep indoor air clean, and avoid physical exertion outdoors.
