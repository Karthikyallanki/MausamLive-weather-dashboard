# Ultraviolet (UV) Index & Solar Protection Guidance

## What is the UV Index?
The Ultraviolet (UV) Index is an international standard measurement of the strength of sunburn-producing ultraviolet radiation at a particular place and time. It ranges from 0 (minimal exposure hazard) to 11+ (extreme risk of harm from unprotected sun exposure).

## UV Index Risk Categories & Action Scale

### Low (UV 0 - 2)
- **Risk Level:** Minimal danger for the average person.
- **Action Required:** Minimal protection required. Wear sunglasses on bright days.

### Moderate (UV 3 - 5)
- **Risk Level:** Moderate risk of harm from unprotected sun exposure.
- **Action Required:** Wear SPF 30+ sunscreen, a broad-brimmed hat, and sunglasses. Seek shade around midday (11 AM to 4 PM).

### High (UV 6 - 7)
- **Risk Level:** High risk of skin and eye damage.
- **Action Required:** Generously apply SPF 30+ broad-spectrum sunscreen every 2 hours. Wear protective clothing, broad hat, and UV-blocking sunglasses. Reduce time in direct sun between 10 AM and 4 PM.

### Very High (UV 8 - 10)
- **Risk Level:** Very high risk. Unprotected skin and eyes can burn rapidly (often in under 15 minutes).
- **Action Required:** Take extra precautions. Avoid outdoor exposure during peak daylight hours. Shirt, sunscreen, hat, and sunglasses are essential.

### Extreme (UV 11+)
- **Risk Level:** Extreme risk of tissue damage.
- **Action Required:** Take all precautions. Unprotected skin can burn within minutes. Try to avoid sun exposure between 10 AM and 4 PM.
