# Temperature, Humidity & "Feels Like" Heat Index

## Why Does It Feel Hotter Than the Actual Temperature?
The "Feels Like" temperature (or Heat Index / Apparent Temperature) combines ambient air temperature with relative humidity to quantify human thermal discomfort.

When relative humidity is high:
- Sweat cannot evaporate efficiently from skin surfaces.
- Evaporative cooling (the body's natural heat loss mechanism) slows down.
- Consequently, the body retains heat and feels significantly warmer than the measured ambient temperature.

## Heat Index Danger Thresholds
- **27°C - 32°C (Caution):** Fatigue possible with prolonged exposure and activity.
- **32°C - 41°C (Extreme Caution):** Heat cramps and heat exhaustion possible.
- **41°C - 54°C (Danger):** Heat exhaustion likely; heatstroke possible with continued activity.
- **Above 54°C (Extreme Danger):** High risk of heatstroke.

## Cold Weather & Wind Chill
Similarly, strong winds make cold air feel significantly colder by sweeping away the thin boundary layer of warm air insulation surrounding human skin (Wind Chill factor).
